# WHO Sexually Transmitted Infections — Key Facts

## Overview

More than 1 million sexually transmitted infections are acquired every day
worldwide, the majority of which are asymptomatic. Eight pathogens are linked
to the greatest incidence of STIs. Four of these are currently curable:
syphilis, gonorrhoea, chlamydia and trichomoniasis.

## Symptoms

Many sexually transmitted infections produce no symptoms at all. When
symptoms do occur they may include unusual discharge, burning during
urination, sores or ulcers, and pelvic or abdominal pain. Because
asymptomatic infection is common, the absence of symptoms does not mean an
infection is absent.

## Testing and diagnosis

Laboratory testing is the only way to confirm whether a sexually transmitted
infection is present. A risk assessment cannot establish infection status.
Testing is recommended for anyone who has had a new partner, multiple
partners, or unprotected sex, and periodic screening is advised for people
with ongoing risk factors.

## Prevention

Correct and consistent condom use is highly effective at reducing the
transmission of sexually transmitted infections, though it does not remove
risk entirely. Vaccination is available for hepatitis B and for human
papillomavirus. Pre-exposure prophylaxis reduces the risk of acquiring HIV
for people at substantial risk.

## Partner notification

When an infection is diagnosed, informing recent sexual partners so they can
also be tested is an important part of care. Health services can support this
process confidentially.
